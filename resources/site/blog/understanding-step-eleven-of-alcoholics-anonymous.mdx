Step Eleven of Alcoholics Anonymous is about developing a regular practice of prayer and meditation. Its purpose is not to achieve perfect calm or find certainty about every decision. It is to deepen spiritual awareness, seek helpful direction and become more willing to act with honesty, patience and care.

For some AA members, this involves a traditional understanding of God. Others relate to a Higher Power as the AA fellowship, nature, shared human wisdom or a source of values beyond their immediate impulses. Step Eleven leaves room for personal understanding, making it possible to approach the Step without pretending to hold beliefs you do not have.

## What is Step Eleven of Alcoholics Anonymous?

Step Eleven says:

> “Sought through prayer and meditation to improve our conscious contact with God as we understood Him, praying only for knowledge of His will for us and the power to carry that out.”

In everyday language, this means pausing regularly to seek guidance rather than being driven only by fear, resentment or immediate desire. Prayer may involve expressing hopes, asking for direction or reflecting on what is right. Meditation generally involves becoming quiet enough to notice thoughts and feelings without automatically acting on them.

The phrase “as we understood Him” is important. AA has spiritual language and roots, but members arrive with many different religious, spiritual and non-religious perspectives. You do not need to copy another person’s beliefs to begin exploring Step Eleven.

If you are unfamiliar with how this Step fits into the wider programme, [the Twelve Steps of Alcoholics Anonymous explained simply](/blog/the-twelve-steps-of-alcoholics-anonymous-explained) provides a newcomer-friendly overview.

## What is the purpose of AA Step 11?

The earlier Steps encourage people to recognise a problem, accept help, examine harmful patterns, make amends where appropriate and develop a daily recovery practice. Step Eleven helps support that continuing growth.

Its purpose can include:

- creating space between an emotion and a reaction;
- becoming more aware of motives and behaviour;
- asking for guidance rather than trying to control everything;
- developing patience, humility and willingness;
- finding steadiness during uncertainty;
- identifying the next constructive action.

Step Eleven is not a promise that difficult feelings will disappear. Nor does it suggest that every thought arising during prayer or meditation is a message that must be followed. Members often discuss important decisions with a sponsor—a more experienced AA member who offers guidance through the Steps—or with other trusted people.

## Prayer and meditation: what is the difference?

AA members sometimes describe prayer as speaking and meditation as listening. That is a useful starting point, although the practices can overlap.

### Prayer in Step Eleven

Prayer does not have to be formal, eloquent or lengthy. It might be a few sincere words asking for patience, courage or clarity. A person might also express gratitude or ask for help accepting something they cannot control.

The wording of Step Eleven places emphasis on seeking knowledge of the right course and the strength to follow it. This differs from asking for every situation to turn out exactly as preferred.

A simple prayer might focus on questions such as:

- What is the kind and honest thing to do?
- Where am I trying to force an outcome?
- What responsibility can I take today?
- Can I be willing to ask for help?

People with an established faith may use prayers from their own tradition. Others may speak in their own words, sit with a clear intention or write a short reflection. There is no need to manufacture a spiritual experience.

### Meditation in Step Eleven

Meditation can be as simple as sitting quietly and paying attention to the breath for a few minutes. Thoughts will still arise. The aim is not to empty the mind, but to notice thoughts without becoming completely caught up in them.

You could try this basic practice:

1. Sit somewhere reasonably quiet and safe.
2. Set a timer for two to five minutes.
3. Notice the natural movement of your breathing.
4. When your attention wanders, gently return it to the breath.
5. At the end, consider one useful action for the day.

Some people prefer walking meditation, quiet time in nature, reflective reading or guided audio without a religious emphasis. Consistency matters more than duration. If silence feels uncomfortable, begin with one minute rather than forcing a long session.

Meditation can sometimes bring difficult emotions into awareness. If a practice feels overwhelming, stop and speak with an appropriate healthcare professional, therapist or trusted recovery supporter. Meditation should not be used to replace mental health treatment or other necessary care.

## How to practise Step Eleven each day

Step Eleven can become part of an ordinary routine rather than a special event. Morning and evening pauses are common in AA, but the best time is one you can use consistently.

### Try a short morning practice

Before checking messages or starting work, take a few quiet minutes. You might:

- ask for guidance for the day;
- breathe slowly and notice your current state;
- identify where you may need patience;
- choose one recovery-supporting action;
- note a meeting, phone call or commitment you intend to keep.

The aim is to prepare for the day, not predict or control it.

### Pause when emotions rise

Prayer and meditation are not limited to calm moments. A brief pause can be especially useful when you feel angry, frightened, restless or tempted to drink.

Try stopping before replying to a message, making a major decision or entering an argument. Take several breaths, ask what action would support sobriety and contact another person if necessary. Step Eleven works well alongside practical tools for [staying sober one day at a time](/blog/how-to-stay-sober-one-day-at-a-time).

If you are at immediate risk of drinking, prayer or meditation alone may not be enough. Contact a sponsor, supportive friend, AA member, treatment provider or local crisis service, depending on what is happening.

### Review the day without attacking yourself

An evening reflection can help you learn from the day. Consider:

- Where was I honest and helpful?
- Did fear or resentment influence my behaviour?
- Is there something I need to put right?
- What am I grateful for?
- What support do I need tomorrow?

This is not an invitation to rehearse every mistake or condemn yourself. The purpose is awareness followed by appropriate action.

## What if I do not believe in God?

You can be honest about uncertainty. Many people enter AA feeling uncomfortable with religious language because of personal beliefs, past experiences or a lack of belief. You do not have to claim certainty you do not possess.

It may help to focus initially on the practical elements of Step Eleven: becoming quiet, noticing self-centred or fearful thinking, seeking wisdom and choosing actions consistent with recovery. Some people direct their reflections towards a Higher Power of their own understanding. Others think in terms of conscience, reality, the collective experience of the fellowship or principles such as honesty and compassion.

Listening to how different members interpret the Step may reveal approaches that feel more accessible. A sponsor can share personal experience, but should not need to dictate your beliefs. AA groups can also vary in tone, so trying another meeting may be worthwhile if one group does not feel suitable.

AA is not the only route to recovery. Professional treatment, counselling, medication where clinically appropriate, peer-support alternatives and community services may also form part of a person’s recovery plan.

## Common difficulties with Step Eleven

### Expecting an immediate spiritual experience

Step Eleven often develops quietly. Greater patience, a willingness to apologise or the ability to pause before reacting may be more meaningful than a dramatic experience.

### Trying to interpret every thought as guidance

Thoughts can come from anxiety, habit, wishful thinking and many other sources. For important decisions, allow time and consult trustworthy people. Sound guidance is generally consistent with honesty, responsibility and care for others.

### Turning meditation into another test

A wandering mind does not mean you have failed. Noticing that attention has wandered and returning gently is part of meditation.

### Using spirituality instead of practical action

Prayer can support action, but it does not make action unnecessary. Recovery may also require attending meetings, calling someone, making amends, setting boundaries, keeping healthcare appointments or leaving an unsafe situation.

If you may be physically dependent on alcohol, seek medical guidance before stopping suddenly. Abrupt withdrawal can be dangerous for some people, and Step Eleven is not a substitute for professional assessment or supervised care.

## How Step Eleven leads into Step Twelve

Step Eleven encourages an inner practice of spiritual awareness; Step Twelve turns continuing growth towards service and everyday conduct. The patience and guidance cultivated here can help a person support others without trying to control them.

This does not mean becoming an expert. Service might involve welcoming a newcomer, helping with meeting tasks or being available for a conversation. ## Conclusion

Step Eleven of Alcoholics Anonymous is a daily invitation to pause, seek guidance and act on sound principles. Prayer and meditation can remain simple, personal and honest. Over time, a few quiet minutes—supported by meetings, trusted people and practical recovery action—may help create a steadier way of responding to life.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to be religious to practise Step Eleven?

No. Step Eleven uses spiritual language, but AA members understand it in different ways. You can begin with quiet reflection, meditation and a willingness to seek help beyond your immediate impulses. You do not need to pretend to hold a particular belief.

### How long should I meditate for Step Eleven?

There is no required length. One or two minutes can be a realistic beginning, and you may extend the time gradually. A short daily practice is often more manageable than occasional long sessions.

### Can I use a meditation app for AA Step 11?

Yes, if it helps you establish a safe and consistent practice. Choose material that fits your beliefs and recovery needs. An app can support meditation, but it does not replace human connection, meetings, sponsorship or professional care when needed.

### What should I do if prayer seems unanswered?

Step Eleven does not guarantee a particular result. Instead of looking only for an external sign, consider whether the practice is helping you become more patient, honest or willing to take constructive action. Discuss uncertainty with a sponsor, trusted AA member, faith adviser or another suitable source of support.
