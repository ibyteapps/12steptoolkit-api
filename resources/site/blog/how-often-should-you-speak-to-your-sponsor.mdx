There is no universal rule for how often you should speak to your Alcoholics Anonymous sponsor. Many people find daily contact helpful in early sobriety, while weekly or several-times-a-week conversations may be enough once recovery is more established. The best schedule is one that supports honest Twelve Step work, remains practical for both people and is agreed on clearly.

If you are unsure, ask your sponsor directly: “How often would you like me to contact you?” This simple conversation can prevent confusion and help you build a reliable working relationship.

## What does an AA sponsor do?

In Alcoholics Anonymous, a sponsor is usually an AA member who has experience applying the Twelve Steps in their own life and is willing to help another member do the same. The person receiving that guidance is sometimes called a “sponsee.”

A sponsor may:

- Help you understand and work through the Twelve Steps
- Share how they have handled common recovery challenges
- Encourage honesty, responsibility and participation in AA
- Listen when you are dealing with cravings, resentment, fear or uncertainty
- Help you notice when your thinking may be moving toward isolation or drinking

A sponsor is not a therapist, doctor, emergency responder or manager of your life. Sponsorship is peer support based on shared recovery experience. It can be an important part of sobriety, but AA is not the only route to recovery, and a sponsor should not replace professional healthcare or other appropriate support.

If you do not yet have a sponsor, this guide explains [how to ask someone to sponsor you in AA](/blog/how-to-ask-someone-to-sponsor-you).

## How often should you call your sponsor in early sobriety?

Daily contact is commonly suggested during the first days or months of sobriety. This does not mean every call must be long or intense. A brief check-in can help you practise reaching out before difficulties become crises.

For example, you might call or message to say:

- How you are feeling today
- Whether you attended or plan to attend an AA meeting
- Whether you are experiencing cravings or thoughts of drinking
- What action you are taking on a Step or recovery suggestion
- What you are grateful for or finding difficult

Early recovery often involves major changes in routines, relationships and emotions. Regular contact can provide structure while you build a wider support network and learn to respond differently to stress.

However, daily communication is not an official requirement, and it may not be practical in every sponsorship relationship. Time zones, work, caregiving, disability, family responsibilities and communication preferences all matter. The important point is to establish a dependable rhythm rather than guessing what your sponsor expects.

## How often should you speak once sobriety is more established?

As recovery becomes more stable, some people continue speaking to their sponsor every day. Others move to two or three calls a week, one longer weekly conversation or contact focused mainly on Step work.

A weekly scheduled call may work well when you are:

- Consistently attending meetings or using other recovery support
- Actively applying the Steps in daily life
- Able to recognise warning signs and ask for help
- Connected to sober peers rather than relying on one person
- Maintaining recovery routines without frequent prompting

Length of sobriety alone does not determine how much contact you need. Someone with years of sobriety may benefit from extra calls during bereavement, relationship difficulties, illness, job loss or another major change. Someone newer may have a strong professional and peer-support network and need less frequent sponsor contact.

Think in terms of current need, willingness and useful action—not status or seniority.

## Quality matters as much as frequency

Calling every day is not automatically useful if each conversation avoids the real issues. In the same way, a meaningful weekly conversation may provide more support than several rushed exchanges.

Helpful sponsor contact usually includes honesty and a practical next step. You might discuss a resentment, review written Step work, consider whether an apology is appropriate or identify an AA meeting you can attend. A sponsor may also suggest pausing, reflecting, praying or meditating according to your beliefs, or speaking with a qualified professional about an issue outside the sponsor’s experience.

Being a willing participant matters too. You can learn more about consistent contact, honesty and boundaries in [how to be a good AA sponsee](/blog/how-to-be-a-good-aa-sponsee).

## Agree on expectations early

Sponsors have different approaches. One may ask a new sponsee to call every morning. Another may prefer a planned weekly call with additional contact when needed. Some are comfortable with text messages; others believe important subjects are better discussed by phone or in person.

Ask practical questions such as:

- How often should we normally speak?
- Should I call at a regular time?
- Do you prefer calls, messages, video conversations or meeting in person?
- What should I do if you do not answer?
- What times should I avoid contacting you?
- How will we arrange time for Step work?
- Who else should I contact when you are unavailable?

Neither person needs to be available constantly. Clear expectations protect both the sponsor and the sponsee from avoidable disappointment.

## When should you contact your sponsor sooner?

Do not wait for the next scheduled conversation if you notice warning signs that could threaten your sobriety. Consider contacting your sponsor sooner when:

- You are seriously thinking about drinking
- You have begun isolating or avoiding meetings and recovery contacts
- You are hiding behavior because you expect concern from others
- Anger, fear, shame or resentment is building rapidly
- You are about to make a major decision while highly emotional
- You have stopped following through on recovery commitments
- You have had a lapse or returned to drinking

A useful habit is to call before taking an action you may regret, rather than calling only afterward. If your sponsor does not answer, contact another trusted AA member, attend a meeting or use another safe support option. Your sobriety should not depend on one person always being reachable.

## What if you feel you are calling too much?

Frequent contact is not necessarily a problem, particularly in early recovery. It may become unhelpful, however, if you expect your sponsor to make every decision, provide constant reassurance or remain available at all hours.

Healthy sponsorship should gradually support responsibility rather than dependence. A sponsor can share experience and ask helpful questions, but you remain responsible for everyday decisions. It is also wise to build a broader network that may include AA peers, supportive family or friends, counselors, recovery groups, healthcare professionals and community services.

If you are worried about over-contact, ask honestly: “Is the amount I am calling working for you, or should we agree on a different routine?”

## What if you rarely speak to your sponsor?

Infrequent contact may be perfectly workable if it is intentional and you are continuing with Step work. But it may be worth reviewing the arrangement if weeks pass without communication, you continually avoid difficult subjects or neither person is clear about the purpose of the relationship.

Ask yourself:

- Am I contacting my sponsor only when something has gone badly?
- Are we doing any structured Twelve Step work?
- Do I feel able to speak honestly?
- Does my sponsor usually respond within the expectations we agreed?
- Is this relationship helping me take constructive recovery action?

Sometimes a direct conversation is enough to restart the relationship. In other cases, your needs and your sponsor’s availability may no longer match. It is acceptable to seek different guidance. If that possibility feels uncomfortable, see [when and how to change your AA sponsor](/blog/can-you-change-your-aa-sponsor).

## A practical contact plan

If you want a starting point, try this flexible plan:

### During early sobriety

Aim for a brief daily check-in, plus a planned weekly conversation for Step work. Attend AA meetings and collect contact details from other members so that your sponsor is not your only source of support.

### During steadier recovery

Arrange at least one purposeful conversation each week or another schedule that you both consider effective. Increase contact when facing cravings, major stress or important Step work.

### Review the plan regularly

Every month or two, ask whether the current rhythm is helping. Contact may need to increase, decrease or change format as circumstances develop.

This is a practical example, not an AA rule. Sponsorship arrangements vary across groups, cultures and individuals.

## Keep medical and urgent needs separate

A sponsor should not assess withdrawal risk, prescribe treatment or manage a medical emergency. Abruptly stopping alcohol can be dangerous for some people who have become physically dependent. If you are concerned about withdrawal, alcohol use disorder, medication, physical symptoms or mental health, seek guidance from a qualified healthcare professional or local alcohol and drug support service.

If you may harm yourself or someone else, have severe withdrawal symptoms, or face another immediate danger, contact local emergency services rather than waiting for a sponsor to respond.

## Conclusion

There is no single correct number of sponsor calls. Daily contact can provide valuable structure in early sobriety, while weekly or several-times-a-week conversations may work well later. Agree on expectations, focus on honest and useful communication, and reach out sooner when your sobriety feels vulnerable.

Most importantly, build a recovery network rather than relying entirely on one person. A sponsor can be a steady guide through the Twelve Steps, but lasting support often comes from several healthy connections and resources.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to call my AA sponsor every day?

No universal AA rule requires a daily call. Some sponsors recommend it, especially for newcomers, because it builds connection and accountability. Ask your sponsor what they suggest and agree on a routine that is realistic for both of you.

### How long should a sponsor call last?

There is no required length. A daily check-in might take only a few minutes, while a scheduled discussion about Step work could take longer. The conversation should be focused enough to support honest reflection and practical action.

### Should I call my sponsor whenever I have a craving?

It is sensible to reach out when cravings feel difficult, ideally before you are close to drinking. If your sponsor is unavailable, call another recovery contact, attend an AA meeting or use another appropriate support service. Seek professional or emergency help when safety is at risk.

### Is texting my sponsor enough?

Texting can be useful for brief updates or arranging a call, depending on your agreement. Sensitive issues, repeated cravings or detailed Step work may be easier to discuss by phone, video or in person. Ask your sponsor which communication methods work best.
